Sources. Turner's Comparative Dictionary as digitized in JAMBU (Arora et al. 2023); Pischel, Grammatik der Prakrit-Sprachen (1900), in Subhadra Jhā's translation; Oberlies, Pāli (2001); IE-CoR (Heggarty et al. 2023).

Morphology. Each mark gives its rule. 3sg verb ending, 3sg -ai, final anusvāra, and infinitive are the rules used before a fit. Suffix marks come from Turner's own word families: forms of one language under one headword that share a stem show what the suffixes are, and a suffix is cut only if the stem then fits the ancestor better. Each stem is then aligned alone, and so is each member of a compound. Ending -am and the like are a table of Sanskrit endings, applied to words cited without a hyphen, and they overmark: a short noun can pass as inflected. Marked in review came from the reviewers.

Status. Reviewed pairs show the reviewed alignment. Excluded in review pairs were dropped from the fits. Uncertain rows are set aside from their tables and listed under Status. Everything else is the machine alignment.
